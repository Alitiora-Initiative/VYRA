# VYRA Partner, Regulation and Infrastructure Research Register

**Research cut-off:** 26 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Capture the key primary-source references behind the brokerage master plan. The register separates law and regulator evidence from provider marketing and unresolved commercial questions.

## 1. Research and evidence rules

1. Regulator registers and legislation outrank broker/vendor marketing pages.
2. A public regulator announcement demonstrates a category or precedent; it does not grant VYRA permission.
3. Provider material describes capabilities, not a signed contract, Kenya eligibility or legal approval.
4. Country coverage must be confirmed in writing for VYRA's legal entity, the customer's residence, product and selected account model.
5. Any fee, capital threshold, licence category or regulatory timing must be checked against current law and the regulator's current schedule before forecasting.
6. Record the source date and date checked. Re-check before launch and whenever a regulator/provider issues a material update.

### Key Kenya findings

- The 2023 regulations define an intermediary services platform as an electronic application facilitating marketing and distribution of approved collective investment schemes; the provider acts as an intermediary between fund managers and investors.
- Those rules state that a person may not operate an ISPP without a licence. The application information includes a licensed-fund-manager agreement, business plan, adequate resources, fit-and-proper people, platform capacity and security, user terms, functionality, BCP/DR, recordkeeping/audit trail, risk/fraud controls, data protection, AML/CTF, cybersecurity, complaints and risk management.
- The rules contemplate approval before a licensed ISPP markets and distributes collective investment schemes from multiple fund managers.
- The AIF framework includes hedge funds and requires CMA approval. The published 2023 framework restricts public invitations to subscribe and specifies manager governance and experience requirements. VYRA must not create an open retail hedge-fund subscription funnel without a jurisdiction-specific legal opinion.
- CMA's November 2026 announcement shows a local digital platform example where investor verification and platform records are distinct from execution, asset management and custody retained by licensed firms. It is an avenue to investigate, not permission to reproduce that model.
- CMA's current register is the starting point for partner verification; check entity, category, activity scope and current status.

## 5. Candidate regulated brokerage infrastructure

### Interactive Brokers (IBKR)

Official sources:
- Introducing broker programme: https://www.interactivebrokers.com/support/faq/content/get-started-as-introducing-broker-150344495
- Account Management API: https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-api-page/web-api-account-management/
- Registration options: https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/web-api/account-management/resources/registration-options
- Introducing broker onboarding course: https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/trading-course/getting-started-for-introducing-brokers/

Public material describes introducing-broker and registered-adviser workflows, account applications, account maintenance, funds/banking tasks, reports and client tools. This makes IBKR a candidate for a direct account relationship and approved introducing model, rather than a reason to build custody ourselves.

Must be confirmed in writing: whether a Kenyan-incorporated company can enter the proposed model; required CMA or foreign status; customer countries and categories; legal entity that contracts with customer; products and permissions; account minimums and operational requirements; referral or fee terms; branding, portal and API access; market-data costs; and who handles complaints and data protection.

### Saxo

Official sources:
- Introducing broker: https://www.saxoportal.com/institutional-and-partners/introducing-broker.html
- White-label / institutional: https://www.saxoimf.com/institutional-and-partners/white-label.html

Public partner information distinguishes introducing-only, order-assistance and discretionary models. Its institutional pages describe multi-asset execution and post-trade, custody, reporting and APIs. This makes Saxo a candidate for a longer-term multi-asset outsourcing relationship.

Must be confirmed: whether a pure referral model is available to VYRA; licence requirement by activity and country; Kenya resident eligibility; account and custody structure; minimum scale; platform/API and data pricing; onboarding allocation; and brand/marketing approval.

### Alpaca Broker API

Official sources:
- Broker API overview: https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-broker-api
- Broker platform: https://alpaca.markets/broker-api
- Availability outside the US: https://alpaca.markets/support/is-alpaca-available-outside-the-us

Public documentation describes brokerage account opening, funding, trading, clearing and custody with operating models such as fully disclosed and omnibus. Current platform marketing makes scale and country coverage claims. These claims are provider statements and do not prove VYRA's eligibility or Kenya-specific customer coverage.

Must be confirmed: VYRA partner entity eligibility; Kenyan-resident acceptance; the actual regulated contracting entity; licensing requirements imposed on VYRA; products and country availability; account/funding roles; costs, minimums, data rights and production certification.

### DriveWealth

Official sources:
- Developer overview: https://developer.drivewealth.com/apis/docs/intro
- Developer portal: https://developer.drivewealth.com/
- Account opening: https://developer.drivewealth.com/apis/docs/opening-accounts

Public developer documentation describes an API-first brokerage infrastructure for onboarding, funding, trading, custody, clearing and regulatory reporting. It is a candidate for a narrowly scoped securities service where the partner remains the account provider.

Must be confirmed: exact Kenya/customer coverage; authorised partner model; account ownership; KYC and AML responsibilities; minimums and integration costs; data-processing roles; market-data costs; SLAs; incident support; and production approval process.

### Upvest

Official sources:
- Developer docs: https://docs.upvest.co/
- Operating models: https://docs.upvest.co/operating_models
- Developers: https://upvest.co/developers

The documentation describes three operating models: taking the provider licence for single accounts, bringing the platform's own licence for single accounts, and bringing its own licence for omnibus accounts. It requires the operating model to be established before implementation. It is a useful reference for the legal/technical split, but no assumption should be made about Kenyan entity or customer eligibility.

Must be confirmed: regions, partner entity/customer restrictions, licence model, account and custody responsibilities, instrument coverage, costs, minimum scale and API production approval.

## 7. Questions to ask every partner

1. Which legal entity contracts with VYRA and which entity contracts with each customer?
2. Which regulator licenses that entity and which exact permissions cover the requested product?
3. Can the provider onboard a Kenya-incorporated business as the platform partner?
4. Can it onboard Kenyan-resident users under the proposed account and distribution model?
5. Is VYRA a software supplier, introducer, appointed representative, tied agent, ISPP, broker-dealer or another regulated category?
6. Which licence, registration, notification or regulator approval must VYRA obtain first?
7. Who decides account opening, KYC, AML, sanctions, PEP, source of funds and ongoing monitoring?
8. Who receives deposits, controls withdrawals and holds customer cash and assets?
9. Who accepts orders, routes, executes, clears and settles them?
10. Which products, venues, currencies, order types and customer classifications are supported?
11. What market-data rights are granted, and what exchange fees or restrictions apply?
12. What are setup, monthly, account, order, data, minimum-volume and termination fees?
13. Can users have a VYRA-branded front end, or must they use the provider's official portal?
14. Which API scopes, callbacks, webhooks, sandboxes and certification steps are available?
15. What uptime, incident and customer-support response commitments apply?
16. Which user data may VYRA receive, for what purpose, under which controller/processor relationship and for how long?
17. Does the partner approve all VYRA marketing and referral copy?
18. What compensation is permissible, who pays it, what creates conflicts and what disclosures are required?
19. Who owns complaints and regulatory escalation?
20. What happens to accounts, data and open obligations if the partnership ends?

## 8. Unresolved facts that must not be invented

- No broker/fund partner has confirmed VYRA eligibility or signed a contract.
- No regulator has approved VYRA or classified the exact planned customer journey.
- No VYRA licence, AR/IAR appointment, tied-agent status or financial-promotion approval is active.
- No commercial market-data redistribution contract exists.
- No broker production API credentials exist.
- No real customer KYC, account opening, deposits, withdrawals, custody or live trading are enabled.
- No dedicated VYRA Supabase project exists due the organisation's free-project limit.
- No Render demo service exists because workspace selection is still required.
- No full production build or production deployment certification has been recorded.
