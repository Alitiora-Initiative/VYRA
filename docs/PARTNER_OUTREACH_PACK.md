# VYRA Markets — Partner Outreach Pack

**Status:** Discovery material only  
**Version:** 1.0 · 26 November 2026  
**Use:** Institutional discovery calls, regulatory classification requests and partner due diligence. Do not send customer data, solicit investors or promise volumes/permissions.

## 1. What VYRA is asking for

VYRA is a private software project building a partner-led market-access experience. Its target model is to provide a modern customer interface, neutral product discovery, educational information, approved hand-off and (only where permitted) partner-sourced account visibility. The preferred early structure is for a regulated provider to retain the legal customer account, onboarding/KYC decisions, customer money, custody, execution, clearing and settlement.

VYRA is **not currently a licensed broker, custodian, exchange, fund manager, investment adviser, VASP or appointed representative**. It has no signed brokerage/fund partner, no production broker credentials, no live customers, no customer money and no live execution. Its current trading application is paper-only. It seeks a discovery discussion to understand eligibility, required regulatory route, partner controls, commercial minimums and technical access.

Do not tell a provider that VYRA already has a customer base, regulated status, capital, approved licence, committed AUM, confirmed demand or a deployment-ready production stack.

## 5. Initial inquiry to the Kenyan regulator / counsel

**Subject:** Request for guidance on proposed partner-led digital investment platform activities

Dear Sir/Madam,

We are researching the regulatory requirements for a proposed digital platform, VYRA Markets, being developed as a partner-led technology and market-access interface.

The initial concept is to display general product information and, only where permitted, direct users to the official onboarding process of a licensed provider. We are evaluating separate possible services: (a) neutral information and official link-outs; (b) introductions/referrals and whether any compensation is permitted; (c) a digital interface facilitating marketing and distribution of CMA-approved collective investment schemes; (d) display of partner-sourced account information; (e) future API order transmission; and (f) virtual-asset provider discovery or referral.

The project is currently a prototype. It is not licensed or appointed, does not accept customer funds, does not hold assets, does not perform live execution and has no production partner integration.

We would appreciate guidance on:
1. Which activity categories or licences may apply to each proposed function?
2. Whether an ISPP licence is required for the described CIS discovery, marketing, hand-off or distribution workflow.
3. What restrictions apply to introductions, paid referrals, rankings, lead capture and promotional activity?
4. Which functions may be provided as a technology/outsourcing service to a licensed principal, and what approvals or notifications apply?
5. How the VASP Act and 2026 Regulations treat virtual-asset listings, paid referrals, advertising, wallet connection and exchange/order routing separately from securities/CIS activity?
6. Which current regulations, checklists and application guidance should be used for an initial legal assessment?

We understand that this inquiry is not an application or request for authorisation and that any response does not replace legal advice or a formal approval.

Yours faithfully,

VYRA Markets — project representative

## 6. Discovery-call agenda

1. Introductions and partner's relevant regulated legal entity.
2. Exact activities permitted under its licence and where it may serve customers.
3. Proposed VYRA role: pure technology supplier, introducer, appointed/associated entity, ISPP, or another status.
4. Country/customer eligibility, including Kenyan residents and diaspora users.
5. Account ownership, onboarding, KYC/AML/sanctions and investor classification.
6. Customer money, custody, execution, clearing, settlement and complaints ownership.
7. Product and investor restrictions, including retail versus professional/qualified/accredited investors.
8. API, hosted application, OAuth, webhooks, data rights, sandbox and certification.
9. Marketing and referral approval, compensation and conflict disclosures.
10. Security, data transfers, outsourcing approval, business continuity and incident management.
11. Commercial requirements and minimum scale.
12. Next steps, required evidence and written eligibility response.

## 7. Questions that must receive written answers

- Which exact legal entity will sign the agreement?
- What regulator, licence number and permissions apply?
- Can the partner accept the VYRA contracting entity and Kenya-resident customers?
- What separate authorisation must VYRA obtain?
- Does the partner permit referral, lead capture, marketing, content ranking or paid promotion?
- What compensation models are allowed and what regulatory analysis applies?
- Who is the customer contracting party and where is the official customer account?
- Who performs final KYC/AML decisions and sanctions escalation?
- Who receives and holds customer money/assets and how are they segregated?
- Who accepts and executes orders and handles settlement?
- What investors and products may be offered in each country?
- Who handles complaints and regulatory reports?
- Which data can VYRA see, under what basis, and where is it stored?
- What API scopes, rate limits, webhooks and production certification are available?
- What is the full commercial cost including minimums, exchange data, custody, transactions and support?
- How does the arrangement end and how are customers and data transferred?

## 9. Follow-up and recordkeeping

Every contact gets a partner record containing date, team, named contact (if provided by the partner), source URL, questions asked, response, evidence links, open issues, next action, owner and next review date. Store only business contact details necessary for the relationship. Do not add unverified licence data or claim a partner relationship until the written agreement is executed and any regulatory approval/notification is effective.

After a discovery call, send a written summary back to the partner and ask it to correct any misunderstanding. Keep the response with the provider scorecard. No production integration starts from a sales call or verbal assurance.
